## Why

The vitals strip's fuel gauge shows the fuel aboard but not what the burn the player is considering would cost, although the view already holds that quote and uses it for the burn command's fuel ceiling. `docs/plans/system-hud.md` Surface 1 and link 2a-iv of `docs/plans/system-hud/chain.md` assign the gauge a cost overlay, and the three predecessor links (the preview lift, the stranding notice, the commit-clear) have landed everything it needs, so this link is the overlay alone.

## What Changes

- ADD to `system-console` a requirement for the overlay's content: one partial-height cost extent on the fuel gauge, drawn separately from the fill and start-anchored on the gauge's own tons-to-pixels scale, drawn from the quote's served point cost, with its state copied unchanged from the served `affordable` flag and never decided by comparing the extent with the fill; the unaffordable state adds one full-height mark of fixed, non-zero width and a paint of its own, drawn separately from the extent and the fill and, along the rail's length, wholly inside the rail at every cost (no part before its start or past its end), positioned at the extent's end and pulled back by the same percentage of its own width, whose presence follows the flag, so hue is never the only difference between the two states.
- ADD to `system-console` a requirement for the overlay's lifetime: drawn only for the view's successful, current preview as the view last read it, withdrawn (never marked and kept) otherwise, unchanged while a refresh is in flight (a drawn overlay stays drawn, a withdrawn one stays withdrawn until a refresh succeeds), inheriting every withheld state, resume rule and lag from the owning `system-space-view` requirements by name rather than restating them, with the one added bound that a fuel change of itself changes nothing and causes no request, so the overlay's flag reads the earlier response until the first response requested after the change lands (that request sent within one preview refresh interval of the change). The overlay is one-way dependent on the determination the burn command's fuel ceiling reads: it is never drawn without it, and its absence never changes what another requirement presents for that quote.
- The overlay adds no text, no announcement, no request, no figure, no control, and no colour family beyond amber and zinc (amber for the unaffordable extent, a tone already on the strip's gauges for the affordable one; no red, no blue), and is never animated or transitioned.
- The mounting view supplies the strip a quote only while the view also presents that quote's affordability as text; this capability places no rule on where or how.
- No requirement is MODIFIED anywhere and no other capability takes a delta (RULING 8).

### Decided at the panel (RULINGs 15-19), with the cost of each

- **Q2, a gauge with no data draws no overlay (RULING 15).** The live *The Fuel Cell Shows the Served Fuel Tonnage and Estimated Range Over a Gauge* takes no delta. Cost: a dry fleet that selects a target sees no overlay; its fuel figure reads absent and the command panel says "Not affordable with the fuel aboard", so only the panel's text carries its verdict. The other option would have drawn the cost on an empty rail and forced a MODIFIED block of four scenarios.
- **Q4, paint and the non-colour cue (RULING 16).** The extent is a partial-height band drawn separately from the fill, with no minimum drawn width and no change to the rail; the unaffordable state adds the fixed-size full-height mark; exact classes are `design.md`'s. Paint legibility is judged at the journey as a measurement (the sampled centre pixels of the fill, the extent and the rail are pairwise unequal sRGB values in the scheme rendered) plus a tier-1 class-family assertion. Tier 1 asserts the mark's position (start equals the extent's length in percent, pull-back equals the same percentage of its own width, at costs 0, 100 and 250 t, so a mark with no pull-back fails), its non-zero fixed size and full height, the band's part height, and that the mark's colour tokens differ from the unaffordable band's. Cost: the contrast ratios are recorded and not gated, because the reviewer's figures are hand-computed and no zinc shade already on the gauge was found to meet both surfaces in the dark scheme; the extent-past-fill arrangement and the mark's own sampled paint are judged only if the journey arranges them, else recorded untested; what no tier asserts is the mark's containment as rendered pixels (jsdom does no layout, and the journey has no containment leg) and the rounded cap's clipping at the rail's ends; tier 1 holds the two position components that containment reduces to, and review holds the rest.
- **Q5, the canvas unavailable (RULING 17).** The overlay is drawn by construction, and this link carries no scenario or task for that state (see the out-of-scope list). Cost: the state is unpinned here.
- **Q7, the command panel under a stale quote (RULING 18).** The panel is not touched: after this link a stale unaffordable quote shows an amber, stale-marked panel and no gauge overlay, marked on one surface and withdrawn on the other. No scenario states this; `design.md` records the asymmetry so link 4 inherits it.
- **The biconditional, the text clause and the task cap (RULING 19).** "Present exactly together" with the fuel ceiling is one-way; the affordability-text clause is a condition on what the view supplies and places no rule on the command panel; a non-boolean `affordable` flag is not given a clause; the task list reaches 39 by the architect's sheds, with no split.
